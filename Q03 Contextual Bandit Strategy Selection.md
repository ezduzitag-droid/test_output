# Q03 Contextual Bandit Strategy Selection

This record establishes whether a contextual multi-armed bandit can choose which strategy or model to run given market conditions, and how the bot keeps learning without repeatedly selecting poor or untested strategies. LinUCB and Thompson Sampling are the proven contextual policies — TS with the strongest record, LinUCB the analytic comparator. The hard parts are the plumbing: reward must be point-in-time, delayed, risk-adjusted, net of costs; policies evaluated offline via inverse propensity scoring before live exploration; exploration bounded. Recommendation: Thompson Sampling default, LinUCB comparator, offline replay until component H provides reliable historical evaluation. Affects G, F, H, I, J, K, L.

## Question IDs

- Q03-A — Feasibility and design of a contextual bandit for strategy/model selection, including reward calculation.
- Q03-B — Continuous strategy evaluation and learning under exploration that avoids repeatedly selecting poor or insufficiently tested strategies.

## Original research questions

- Q03-A: "Could our ML bot use a contextual multi-armed bandit to select the most appropriate strategy or predictive model based on current market conditions, and how should it calculate the reward for each selection?"
- Q03-B: "How can the bot continuously evaluate alternative strategies and learn from their performance without allowing its exploration process to repeatedly select poorly performing or insufficiently tested strategies?"

## Technical concepts

- **Multi-armed bandit (MAB):** one arm chosen per step; only that reward observed; goal is maximizing cumulative reward.
- **Contextual bandit:** a context vector (market/regime features) observed before each choice; the best arm may depend on it.
- **Thompson Sampling (TS):** Bayesian policy with a posterior per arm's payoff; sample and play the best sample — exploration via uncertainty.
- **LinUCB:** linear-payoff contextual policy with a ridge regression per arm; plays the highest upper-confidence-bound arm.
- **Softmax / ε-greedy:** baselines — play the empirical best with probability 1−ε, or weight arms by softmax; ε-greedy explores context-blind.
- **Exploration vs exploitation:** trying arms to learn payoffs versus exploiting the best arm.
- **Context features:** pre-decision observables — regime probabilities, entropy, volatility, trend, breadth, liquidity — point-in-time.
- **Reward function:** the chosen strategy's forward return over a fixed horizon, net of transaction costs, optionally risk-adjusted.
- **Delayed rewards:** outcomes realized after the horizon; credited when it completes.
- **Risk-adjusted rewards:** downside-penalizing shapes (vol-scaling, drawdown penalties) so the bandit does not prefer high-variance luck.
- **Off-policy / offline evaluation:** estimating a candidate policy from logged data generated by another policy.
- **Inverse propensity scoring (IPS):** unbiased estimator reweighting logged outcomes by 1/(logging probability).
- **Logged bandit data:** records of (context, arm, propensity, reward); the IPS prerequisite.
- **Safe exploration in trading:** capped exploratory selections, risk-limited arms excluded, no stress-context exploration, posterior-sampling preferred.
- **Nonstationarity:** drifting rewards; sliding-window or discounted posteriors keep policies adaptive at higher variance.

## Research findings

### Q03-A findings

- The contextual bandit formalism maps directly onto strategy selection: observe context, pick one arm, later observe only that arm's forward performance — the defining feedback structure [ESTABLISHED; Sutton & Barto ch. 2, Li et al. (2010)].
- LinUCB is the analytic contextual policy: Li, Chu, Langford and Schapire (2010, WWW) introduced it for personalized news recommendation, validated on real Yahoo! traffic; the disjoint variant keeps per-arm ridge regressions acting on upper confidence bounds [ESTABLISHED]. Its linear-payoff assumption and α knob are awkward with noisy financial rewards [INTERPRETATION].
- Thompson Sampling has the strongest record: Agrawal and Goyal (2013, ICML) proved regret guarantees for TS with linear payoffs [ESTABLISHED]; Chapelle and Li (2011, NIPS) found TS consistently best or tied-best against UCB/LinUCB variants and simpler to tune (one prior) [ESTABLISHED]. LinUCB offers cleaner analytic confidence; TS dominates empirically [CONTESTED] — resolved by implementing both and deciding on logged data.
- Reward is the crux: sources use immediate scalars (clicks). Trading adds delay (outcome known after horizon h), cost (fees netted), risk (raw means favor high-variance arms). Reward must come from data available at realization time, credited to the selection made h bars earlier [INTERPRETATION for the shape, anchored in PIT requirements and H's accounting].
- Context must be point-in-time: Q01 probabilities, Q02 state/entropy, C features as of the decision date; full-sample stats or smoothed labels as context reintroduce look-ahead [ESTABLISHED; PIT principles].

### Q03-B findings

- Continuous evaluation must be offline-first: Li, Chu, Langford and Wang (2011, WSDM) established unbiased offline evaluation via inverse propensity scoring, with IPS rankings tracking online A/B results on Yahoo! traffic [ESTABLISHED]. Prerequisites: propensities logged; IPS weights explode when the logging policy rarely chose a candidate-favored arm; candidates must stay within its support [ESTABLISHED; variance mitigations (clipping, self-normalization) INTERPRETATION].
- Exploration must be bounded and safe: prefer TS-style posterior sampling (explores only where uncertainty is consistent with plausible optimality); impose a hard exploration budget (fraction of non-greedy selections); cap per-arm exposure in L; forbid exploration on arms breaching risk limits or in stress contexts; enforce a minimum-trials floor — untested arms get budgeted trials or stay quarantined [INTERPRETATION; posterior-sampling-as-exploration ESTABLISHED].
- Sutton & Barto's bandit chapter establishes ε-greedy, UCB1, and regret framing making "repeatedly selecting poor arms" measurable [ESTABLISHED]. ε-greedy fits logging/baseline use, not production — context-blind exploration [INTERPRETATION].
- Nonstationarity is the structural risk: rewards per context are assumed stationary; markets drift. Mitigations: discounted/sliding-window posteriors; regime probabilities in context; periodic offline re-evaluation [INTERPRETATION].
- The plan's warning applies directly: a continuously selecting bandit is the "complex selection" barred until reliable historical evaluation exists. Sequence: log propensities from a simple baseline → offline IPS of TS/LinUCB → replay in H → limited live exploration under L's caps [INTERPRETATION; consistent with Gate 1].

## How the technique works

Loop. At date t: observe context x_t (PIT); choose arm a_t by policy π; after horizon h credit reward r_t = f(forward return of a_t over [t, t+h], net of costs, risk-adjusted) to (t, a_t). Only the chosen arm's reward is observed — hence logged propensities and IPS for comparing other arms.

LinUCB (disjoint). Per arm a maintain `A_a = I + Σ x x^T`, `b_a = Σ r x`; select `a_t = argmax_a [θ_a^T x_t + α √(x_t^T A_a^{-1} x_t)]` with `θ_a = A_a^{-1} b_a`. α sets the confidence radius (larger = more exploration).

Thompson Sampling (linear-Gaussian). Maintain a posterior over θ_a; each step sample θ̃_a and play `argmax_a θ̃_a^T x_t`; update the played arm's posterior on reward realization. Exploration falls automatically as posteriors tighten.

Offline evaluation (IPS). With logged (x, a, p, r), `V_IPS = mean[r · 1{a = a_candidate(x)} / p]`; self-normalized variant divides by mean weight. Diagnose 1/p for variance blow-ups.

Inputs: PIT context matrix, arm definitions with net forward-return streams, horizon h, policy parameters. Outputs: per-date selection with propensity, reward credits, posteriors, regret proxy. Strengths: principled exploration, regime-aware conditioning, online learning, safe offline evaluation. Limitations: unchosen arms unobserved; stationarity per context assumed; IPS variance with rare actions; reward shaping easy to get subtly wrong. Computation: O(d²) ridge updates — negligible daily. Failure: context leakage, netting errors, propensity mis-logging, cold arms, posterior collapse, uncapped live exploration.

## Reference implementation sketch

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

class LinUCB:
    """Disjoint LinUCB (Li et al., 2010). PIT-safe: selection at t uses only
    rewards realized strictly before t; delayed rewards credit at completion."""
    def __init__(self, n_arms, dim, alpha=0.5):
        self.A = [np.eye(dim) for _ in range(n_arms)]
        self.b = [np.zeros(dim) for _ in range(n_arms)]
        self.alpha = alpha

    def select(self, x):
        scores = []
        for A, b in zip(self.A, self.b):
            A_inv = np.linalg.inv(A)
            theta = A_inv @ b
            scores.append(float(theta @ x + self.alpha * np.sqrt(x @ A_inv @ x)))
        return int(np.argmax(scores))

    def credit(self, arm, x, r):
        self.A[arm] += np.outer(x, x)
        self.b[arm] += r * x

def replay(contexts, fwd_rets, cost=0.001, horizon=5, alpha=0.5):
    """Offline replay. contexts: T x d PIT features. fwd_rets: T x n_arms
    forward returns; row t is only KNOWN at t+horizon — never read earlier."""
    T, n = fwd_rets.shape
    policy = LinUCB(n, contexts.shape[1], alpha)
    chosen, credited = [], []
    for t in range(T):
        if t >= horizon:                      # credit the reward that matured
            arm0 = chosen[t - horizon]
            r = fwd_rets.iloc[t - horizon, arm0] - cost   # net-of-cost reward
            policy.credit(arm0, contexts.iloc[t - horizon].values, r)
            credited.append(r)
        chosen.append(policy.select(contexts.iloc[t].values))
    s = pd.Series(credited)
    return {"mean_reward": float(s.mean()), "n": len(s),
            "arm_counts": pd.Series(chosen).value_counts().to_dict()}

# Production default: Thompson Sampling; LinUCB stays the comparator. For IPS,
# the logging policy must record propensities (log with TS or epsilon-mixed).
```

## Recommended improvements

1. **P0 — Logging before learning.** Instrument the current selection policy to log (context, arm, propensity, reward at h); without it no unbiased offline evaluation is possible.
2. **P0 — Reward contract.** One shared reward function (net-of-cost, risk-adjusted, credited at window completion) used identically in replay and live; unit-test its timing.
3. **P1 — TS default, LinUCB comparator.** Decide by offline IPS comparison on logged data, not intuition.
4. **P1 — Exploration safety interlocks.** Budget cap, per-arm exposure caps in L, no-exploration stress zones, kill-switch fallback to the best in-sample arm.
5. **P1 — IPS diagnostics.** Report weight distributions and effective sample size; document any clipping bias.
6. **P2 — Nonstationarity handling.** Discounting or sliding-window posteriors with a monitored parameter; re-run evaluation on rolling windows.

## Acceptance criteria

- Causality test: selections at t unchanged when data with availability after t is removed.
- Reward timing test: a selection at t is credited exactly once, at t+h, using only data available at t+h.
- Logging completeness: every selection has context reference, arm, propensity; log passes a schema test.
- Offline evaluation exists: IPS (with diagnostics) comparing TS, LinUCB, and the baseline, reproduced across ≥ 3 rolling windows.
- Exploration bound: non-greedy fraction ≤ the configured budget in replay; enforced in code in any live mode.
- Regression guard: policy versions registry-versioned; each rerun reports cumulative reward vs the fixed baseline (regret proxy).

## Risk assessment

Implementation risks: reward shaping rewarding variance (mitigate with risk adjustment); propensity logging gaps breaking IPS; cold arms; posterior collapse. Operational risks: exploration converting to drawdowns if caps are bypassed; stale posteriors under nonstationarity; delayed-reward bookkeeping errors after restarts (durable ledger in I). Failure modes: repeated selection of a poor-but-recently-lucky arm (bounded by demotion and caps); IPS misleading when candidates leave the logging policy's support (restrict candidates or improve logging). Regressions: context-definition changes invalidate posteriors — version contexts, rerun evaluations end-to-end.

## Implementation decision

Adopt the contextual bandit for G: Thompson Sampling default, LinUCB comparator, one shared net-of-cost risk-adjusted reward, mandatory propensity logging. Deployment is gated: logging → offline IPS → replay in H → bounded live exploration under L's caps. Until H provides reliable historical evaluation, the selector runs in replay/log-only mode — consistent with the plan's warning.
