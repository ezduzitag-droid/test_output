Q25 CORRECTED. I earlier gave PSI rule of thumb "<0.1 stable, 0.1-0.25
moderate, >0.25 large". What the sources actually show:
 - Evidently defaults: KS test for numeric data when <=1000 objects,
   drift flagged when p-value < 0.05; PSI default threshold 0.1
   [S14].
 - Domino describes a generally accepted PSI threshold of 0.2-0.25
   [S15].
 Conclusion: thresholds are tool- and convention-dependent. Pick one,
 write it down, and calibrate on your own stable periods (measure the
 false-alarm rate on data known to be stationary). Treat 0.1 as
 sensitive and 0.2-0.25 as conservative. Also KS p-values become
 over-sensitive on large samples, so combine with an effect-size
 measure (PSI, Wasserstein, Jensen-Shannon, all available in S14).

Q37 REFINED. SEC Rule 15c3-5 applies to broker-dealers that have or
 provide market access; the FAQ states that if a firm does not have or
 provide market access, the rule does not apply [S13]. So it is not a
 binding requirement on an individual trading through a broker, but it
 is a useful reference design: the rule requires controls to be
 pre-trade, under the broker's direct and exclusive control, with
 financial controls limiting exposure and regulatory controls ensuring
 compliance [S13]. Lesson for your bot: keep risk limits outside the
 strategy's control, and enforce them before orders reach the venue.
 Low-authority glossary (questdb.com) lists typical checks: max order
 quantity, price checks, duplicate-order protection, aggregate position
 monitoring; verify against the rule text before citing.
 Exchange kill switches exist as a venue feature; one SEC filing
 (PHLX) describes that after a kill switch triggers, the member must
 contact exchange operations to re-authorize trading
 https://www.sec.gov/file/34-71559. This supports the one-way latch
 design in Q38 (re-enable requires an explicit, authenticated human
 step).

Q35 (execution realism) now partly verified:
 - Empirical square-root impact law: average price impact of a large
   order scales as sigma * sqrt(Q/V) (volatility times the square root
   of order size over volume) [S16]. Reported across stocks, futures,
   options, and (per a secondary review) crypto.
 - But the exponent is not settled: Almgren et al. found a 3/5 power
   law for temporary impact in their equity data [S17]; other work
   proposes logarithmic dependence (cited in the Cambridge chapter
   https://cambridge.org/core/books/trades-quotes-and-prices/impact-of-metaorders/7320C5E965EE6CE7B755E36FAA542899).
 - IMPLEMENT: slippage = a + b * sigma * (Q/ADV)^delta with delta
   calibrated from YOUR fills (start 0.5, test 0.6), never assumed.
   Small retail-size orders may sit in a regime where spread and fees
   dominate and impact is negligible; check with your own size/ADV.
 - VALIDATE: fit delta on paper-trade fills (Q36); compare residuals.

Q43 / Q44 (circuit breakers) now verified:
 - Closed -> Open when failures in a time window exceed a threshold;
   Open -> Half-Open after a timeout; Half-Open lets limited requests
   through and closes on success, reopens on failure [S18].
 - Microsoft states the breaker serves a different purpose than retry;
   you can combine them by retrying through a breaker [S18].
 - Documented risks: breaker stuck open too long keeps rejecting after
   the fault is fixed; switching to half-open too fast causes
   oscillation [S18]. Plus the AWS caution (first block, S11) that
   breakers add modal behavior that is hard to test.
 - IMPLEMENT: per-dependency breaker, increasing open timeout, small
   half-open probe count, state-change events logged to audit (Q40) and
   health (Q41). Count timeouts, connection errors, 429/5xx as
   failures; do not count your own 4xx bugs.

Q33 (reproducibility) now verified:
 - MLflow can log datasets as run inputs with name, digest, schema, and
   source, giving lineage from data to model [S19].
 - DVC works on top of Git, tracks data/models/pipelines, supports
   parameter-level dependencies in dvc.yaml and experiment runs [S20].
 - Caveat: a research review notes AutoML platforms don't give
   reproducibility out of the box (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14325);
   test it (replay job), don't assume it.
 - Check project status of DVC after the reported lakeFS acquisition

   snapshots.

Q19 (validation tooling) now verified:
 - pandera: schema defined once, checks on column types/properties,
   statistical hypothesis tests, lazy validation (collect all failures
   before raising), data synthesis for property-based tests [S21].
 - Great Expectations is an alternative (confirmed to exist and be

   https://pypi.org/project/adc-toolkit/); I did not open its own docs.
 - IMPLEMENT: pandera DataFrameModel for candle schema with checks for

   run reports every violation.

Q06 (exploration control) refined by verified source:
 - Successive Halving allocates budget to many configurations, drops
   the worst fraction each round, repeats; Hyperband hedges across
   several halving brackets [S22].
 - CAUTION [from the DEHB paper snippet]: SH can discard configurations
   at low budgets that would have been best at the highest budget.
   For trading, this means strategies whose edge needs a long sample
   (rare events, slow regimes) can be culled wrongly. Mitigate: set the
   minimum evaluation budget by the strategy's trade frequency, and
   keep a small protected slot for slow-edge candidates.
 - SH/Hyperband are for hyperparameter/configuration search under a
   fixed budget; applying them to live strategy culling is my
   adaptation [A].
